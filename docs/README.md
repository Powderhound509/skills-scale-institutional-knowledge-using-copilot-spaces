# OctoAcme Project Management Documentation

OctoAcme uses a customer-first, iterative lifecycle that moves work from initiation and validation through planning, execution and tracking, risk and communication management, release and deployment, and retrospective continuous improvement. Teams define the business problem, measurable outcomes, stakeholders, initial risks, and resource needs before approving an initiative for planning. Approved work is broken into prioritized, testable increments with acceptance criteria, estimates, owners, dependencies, milestones, and a release plan.

Project Managers coordinate schedules, risks, dependencies, meetings, documentation, and stakeholder communication. Product Managers and Product Leads define the product vision and success measures, prioritize the backlog, and make trade-off decisions. Developers implement and test the solution, contribute to planning and reviews, and identify technical risks. QA and testing participants validate acceptance criteria and product quality, while stakeholders provide input, approvals, and feedback throughout the lifecycle.

Teams keep work visible on a shared project board and use a project README, status report, or release documentation as a shared source of truth. Daily standups focus on progress, blockers, and dependencies; weekly delivery and project syncs review progress, risks, and decisions; demos occur at sprint or milestone boundaries; and stakeholders receive weekly, milestone-based, or monthly updates as appropriate. Blockers are escalated from the delivery team through the Project Manager and Product Lead to the sponsor when business impact requires it.

Quality is built into delivery with unit tests for new logic, applicable integration tests, and end-to-end smoke tests for critical flows. CI runs automated tests, linting, and security scans, and manual QA supplements automation when feature acceptance needs it. Releases require acceptance criteria, release notes, smoke tests, rollback or mitigation plans, staging validation, and post-release verification. Retrospectives after sprints, releases, milestones, and incidents turn lessons into owned, time-bound improvements.
